# Editorial log: Saturday 3 October 2026, morning edition (no. 17)

**Lead: the DTU hack.** Seven scoring publishers carried it, more than anything else in the window. DTU's own release (via Ritzau) is attached as primary material. The scorer ranked it second, behind the defence minister's escape from censure. I put it first because it has the facts of record: a breach of the identity system, "a large amount of data" taken, up to 200,000 people affected. An Aarhus professor and a DR expert supply the named judgements.

**Secondaries.** *Bruus spared a censure*: this is the day's development in the Borris thread. The support parties refused a næse and the prime minister said she trusts Defence. It is politics, not new facts, so it runs second, not as a lead. *G7 releases 100 million barrels*: a decision with a Danish diesel-price angle, given by a Danske Bank analyst. It opens a new thread, fuel-price-crisis. *France's school protests*: five outlets, about 560 schools disrupted. *The Hole brothers' fine*: six outlets, and KD's lawyer calls the ruling principled.

**Briefs.** Finance-bill week: the daycare training money, the ferry municipalities' plea, rural transport and Rejsekort on Bornholm, blue backing for a lower EU budget, and the packaging-deal row. Also the gas-boiler target, both Greenland items, Risskov, Siddique, and Syd for Solen as the only culture item. Abroad: Sweden's second attempt, US jobs, the Farage charge, Zelensky's claim (it must be attributed and marked unverified), Ebola, and Danish Crown.

**Departures.** The Danish section held 19 of the top 24. Every world item came from outside_budget to keep that section under half the page. In exchange I dropped ten eligible Danish items: a pig-lorry crash, a road-killed wolf, the Nyborg fire, and several single crimes and accidents.

**Left out as covered.** The Borris report itself ran yesterday, and today's pieces are analysis of the same event. Christa Pike on a ventilator, and the prosecutors' symbols ban, have no material development.

**Felt wrong.** The ranking rewards breadth among regional crime items over world events that Danish outlets plainly made news. The G7 story reached rank 74 with five publishers and peak prominence. Separately, the Nanortalik killing and the Kujalleq alcohol ban are in the same municipality, but no source links them and the copy must not either.
