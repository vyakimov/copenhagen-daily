Monetary policy · Nationalbanken

Nationalbanken holds, and warns the krone peg will be defended “at any price”

Governor Christian Kettel Thomsen left the key rate unchanged at 2.1 per cent and used unusually direct language about speculative pressure on the currency.

“We have the reserves, we have the mandate, and we have done this before.”
Christian Kettel Thomsen, quoted by Børsen

The decision had been widely expected after the ECB's pause last week, but the tone was not. The bank's statement referred three times to “orderly conditions” and once to “unlimited intervention”, phrases it has not used since 2015.

Børsen reports that reserves rose by 14 billion kroner in August, the largest monthly increase since the pandemic. The FT notes that hedge funds have reopened positions against the peg for the first time in a decade, citing Denmark's fiscal surplus as the reason a revaluation, not a devaluation, is the trade.

Politiken's economics desk describes the language as a message to Frankfurt as much as to markets: Denmark will not follow a faster ECB easing cycle if it puts the currency under strain.

The next scheduled decision is on 22 October. The bank does not pre-announce intervention, and the governor declined to say whether the phrase “any price” included an emergency rate rise.
